**Full reference:** Mercier, H. (2020). *Not Born Yesterday: The Science of Who We Trust and What We Believe*. Princeton University Press.

**Usage in section:** Introduces epistemic vigilance—the capacity to evaluate testimony by reasoning about who speaks and why. Names what AI systems lack: the ability to track expected positions, costs of deviation, and what deviation signals.

**Function:** Develops the specific mechanism (epistemic vigilance) that the Liberal approach requires. This is the application of Mercier's framework to the paper's argument.

### Mercier & Sperber (2017)

**Full reference:** Mercier, H., & Sperber, D. (2017). *The Enigma of Reason*. Harvard University Press.

**Usage in section:** Confirmation bias as functional in collective contexts—people marshal evidence for their positions, but others challenge weak arguments. The bias becomes pathological only without challenge. Applied to sycophancy analysis.

**Function:** Grounds the sycophancy connection. Shows that accommodation/confirmation bias has the same structure as source-attending: functional with challenge, pathological without.

**Relation to Complete Prompt:** Already introduced in Section 5 for general theory of reason. Here used for specific claim about confirmation bias.

**Quality notes:** Primary source; foundational text. Second use in paper (first was Section 5).
