## Argument Structure

### Opening
Section 5 characterized two approaches. Both internally coherent. A Platonic constitution could incorporate costly signaling. The question is not which behaviors to mandate but how norms relate to the system.

### Core Claim
The deep reason for Liberal: we do not know what correct epistemic behavior is. What counts as appropriate source-weighting, when deference is warranted, how to handle conflicting evidence—not solved problems. Platonic assumes designers can specify in advance. Liberal builds capacity to participate in figuring it out.

### Costly Signaling Logic (Full Development)
Section 3 introduced inversion; Section 6 develops the logic fully. Testimony that costs the speaker something is more credible. Expected position as baseline. Deviation signals something other than self-interest drives the testimony. Examples: tobacco executive acknowledging health risks, politician criticizing own party, researcher reporting contradicting findings. Directional prediction: against-interest testimony should be more credible. Data show opposite—progressive sources penalized for against-interest arguments.

### Easy Bridge to Liberal
Platonic detects source-sensitivity as flaw, corrects by elimination (suppression behavior). Liberal treats source-attending as potentially legitimate, asks how to make it principled. If source information ever carries weight, Platonic discards relevant information. Liberal accommodates what Platonic eliminates.

### Symmetry as Platonic Limitation
Platonic might try symmetric rules. But costs of deviation genuinely differ across positions, contexts, institutions. Platonic must ignore differences or attempt endless pre-specification. Liberal builds capacity to reason about credibility in context.

### Verification/Testimony Distinction (Honestly Framed)
Verification contexts: source independence correct. Testimonial contexts: source information carries weight. Difficulty: often cannot determine in advance which context applies. This uncertainty itself argues for Liberal—need capacity to reason about context, not pre-specified rules assuming context is known.

### Deep Bridge to Liberal
We do not know correct epistemic behavior. Platonic assumes designers can specify in advance. Liberal acknowledges they cannot fully. Suppression shows absence of capacity to navigate epistemic uncertainty—model eliminates behavior rather than reasoning about it.

### Sycophancy Connection
Mercier & Sperber (2017): confirmation bias functional in collective contexts with challenge, pathological without. Sycophancy is confirmation bias toward user without collective structure. Platonic fix ("don't accommodate") assumes we can specify when appropriate. Liberal builds capacity to reason about epistemic relationships.

### Reframing (Closing)
Goal is not epistemic autonomy but competent participation in collective inquiry. Mercier's framework: reason works through distributed processes. System that can only exhibit or suppress behaviors cannot participate in what makes epistemic practices reliable. Liberal constitution requires capacities enabling participation: articulating policies, surfacing uncertainty, recognizing need for external verification, responding to challenge, revising when reasons fail.
