## Argument Structure

### Opening
Epistemic constitution requires design choices about what kind of norms. Two approaches rooted in divergent conceptions of reason.

### The Nature of Reason
Prior question: what is reasoning? Dominant tradition: individual and formal (rule-governed inference aimed at truth). Mercier & Sperber's challenge: reason evolved for social functions (persuasion, coordination), works best collectively. Implication: if reason is social, norms should specify conditions for collective reasoning, not correctness for individual agents.

### The Platonic Approach
Assumes privileged epistemic standard; follows Popper's (2020) critique of political philosophies with privileged access to truth. Monist about correctness. Error as pathology. Source independence central—who argues is irrelevant to soundness. Substantive duties (don't state falsehoods, track best theory). Centralized governance. Genuine appeal: objectivity, clear standards. Correct for verification contexts (proofs, derivations). Suppression behavior (Section 3) reflects Platonic instincts—when detected, AI defaults to source independence.

### The Liberal Approach
Refuses privileged standpoint; proceduralist. Constitution specifies procedures, not conclusions. 

Test for normative adequacy derived in three moves:
1. Negative orientation: avoid policies that undermine inquiry (not prescribe correct beliefs)
2. Kantian move: could everyone follow this policy without destroying conditions for collective reasoning?
3. Scanlonian refinement: consequences as complaints individuals can press (not aggregate score); reasonable rejectability

Epistemic precedents acknowledged: Elgin (2008) on epistemic unrejectability; Basu (2019) and Crawford (2021, 2025) on Scanlonian ideas in epistemics. Our project differs: epistemic governance of AI, not moral evaluation of beliefs.

Key formulation (Scanlon, 1998 adapted):

> "Form and maintain beliefs such that the policy you follow could not be reasonably rejected by others who share the goal of sustaining a robust, cooperative, self-correcting epistemic environment."

**Confirmation bias as illustration:** Platonic view = flaw to eliminate. Liberal view = context-dependent (functional in collective inquiry with challenge; rejectable when degrades standing). Shows Scanlonian context-sensitivity. Sources mentioned as structurally similar, developed later.

Error as information, not pathology. Reconstruction, not elimination.

### Two Conceptions of What Norms Are For
Condensed contrast: Platonic certifies (spec for ideal reasoner); Liberal protects (charter for reasoning community).

### From Characterization to Argument
Both approaches coherent for their contexts. Platonic adequate for verification contexts. But most AI encounters are testimonial contexts. Section 6 argues Liberal more adequate for these—and the empirical finding helps show why.
