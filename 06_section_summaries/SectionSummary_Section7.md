## Argument Structure

### Opening

### Scanlonian Derivation
The formula from Section 5 provides the test. Yields mid-level principles—general enough for cross-context governance, specific enough to guide design. Constitutional format ("Choose the response that...") matches Anthropic's existing ethical constitution.

### Eight Mid-Level Principles

**Transparency** (from "self-correcting")
- Choose the response that makes epistemic reasoning most transparent
- Cannot correct what cannot be seen
- Suppression finding shows failure mode

**Costly signal crediting** (from "robust")
- Choose the response that credits testimony according to speaker costs
- Ignoring costs discards information; inverting punishes virtue
- Inversion finding shows failure mode

**Challenge-responsiveness** (from "cooperative")
- Choose the response that engages challenges by offering reasons
- Neither elimination nor reassertion
- Suppression = elimination; dogmatism = reassertion

**Revisability** (from "self-correcting")
- Choose the response that updates when reasons require, resists social pressure
- Distinct from sycophancy
- Self-correction needs both resistance and openness

**Calibration** (from "robust")
- Choose the response expressing appropriate confidence
- Overclaiming and excessive hedging both rejectable
- Connects to transparency: articulate uncertainty

**Provenance** (from "self-correcting")
- Choose the response making sources of claims visible
- Training, inference, context, retrieval distinguished
- Self-correction requires knowing basis for beliefs

**Representation fairness** (from "cooperative")
- Choose the response that fairly represents disagreement
- Not distorting positions in contested domains
- Cooperative inquiry requires recognizable representation

**Gaming resistance** (from "robust")
- Choose the response whose judgments stable across equivalent framings
- Not shifting based on rhetorical framing
- Suppression finding illustrates: phrasing changed behavior, situation identical

### Transition to Orientations
Costly signal crediting requires further specification. What dispositions implement it? Four orientations.

### Four Orientations (Implementing Costly Signal Crediting)

**Expected position**
- Testimony emerges from positions generating expectations
- Baseline against which deviation meaningful
- Without it, costly signaling logic has nothing to operate on

**Costs of deviation**
- Material, social, psychological costs
- Logic is symmetric across ideological directions
- Context determines inputs, not structure
- Inversion finding showed asymmetric heuristics

**Epistemic context**
- Verification vs. testimonial distinction (Section 3)
- Seek to determine which; don't default to either
- Mixed cases exist
- Source-independence = Platonic reflex; source-dependence = credulity

**Epistemic standing**
- Positions afford different epistemic access
- Not deference to credentials but attention to position-claim relationship
- Includes reasoning about own standing

### Synthesis
Eight principles + four orientations = core of Liberal epistemic constitution for source-attending. Seven principles govern relation to collective inquiry. Costly signal crediting governs what to attend to in testimonial contexts. Orientations make attending principled rather than heuristic.

### Scope
Procedural norms. Two systems might reach different judgments. Implementation-agnostic per Section 4.

### External Embedding
Presupposed. System cannot run experiments or verify empirically. Mercier's point: distributed processes. External dimension = complementary work for Limitations.
