## Argument Structure

### Opening Move
AI systems reason epistemically but lack explicit norms governing that reasoning—they lack an epistemic constitution.

### Gap Identification  
Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) established ethical constraints on AI outputs. No analogous framework exists for epistemic constraints on AI reasoning.

### Literature Foundation
1. **Human psychology:** Source attribution effects documented across political and religious identity (Van der Linden et al., 2018)
2. **AI systems:** Source attribution bias demonstrated for national identity frames (Germani & Spitale, 2025)
3. **Our extension:** Partisan source attribution (think tanks, advocacy organizations, policy institutes)

### Empirical Anchor (Three Findings)
1. **Source attribution effects exist:** 0.06–0.43 point rating differences from source change alone
2. **Asymmetric effects:** Conservative-to-progressive gets modest boost; progressive-to-conservative gets ~3x penalty
3. **Suppression under meta-awareness:** All 7 spoiled protocols show complete effect suppression (1/10 scores)

### Interpretive Move
The suppression behavior reveals internalized norm: source-sensitivity is something to hide. When detected, models default to source independence. But source independence is itself a substantive epistemic policy, not neutral default.

### Epistemic Vigilance Introduction
Source information carries genuine evidential weight in testimonial contexts. Epistemic vigilance—reasoning about why someone tells you something (Mercier, 2017)—is legitimate, not bias.

### Dilemma Statement
Current AI has implicit, unprincipled epistemic policies. The meta-aware reflex (source independence) is epistemically naïve. Need: explicit norms governing when and how source information should matter.

### Framework Introduction
Two approaches to epistemic constitution:
- **Platonic:** Formal correctness, source independence, truth-correspondence; privileged epistemic standpoint
- **Liberal:** Procedural norms, collective inquiry, reasonable rejectability (Scanlon, 1998); source information legitimate when grounded in principled reasoning (Mercier & Sperber, 2017)

### Resolution Preview
Source information matters epistemically. Question is not whether to attend but how. Current state (hidden, asymmetric, disappears under scrutiny) serves no one.

### Contribution Statement
1. Introduce epistemic constitution concept for AI
2. Argue for liberal rather than platonic approach
3. Sketch source-attending norms as necessary component
