## Argument Structure

### Opening
AI systems have implicit epistemic policies that invert the epistemic logic they appear to implement.

### Principled Source-Attending (Mercier's Framework)
Source information matters because it enables reasoning about why we receive a claim. Expected position provides baseline; deviation carries evidential weight. Against-interest testimony is more credible because costly to produce. Examples: progressive think tank arguing fiscal conservatism, defense contractor warning against military expansion.

### The Inversion
AI does the opposite: incoherence reduces rather than increases credibility. Asymmetric penalty (3:1) suggests no principled reasoning—neither costly signaling logic (symmetric boosts) nor coherence-as-credibility (symmetric penalties). Just implicit heuristics.

### Naming the Absence
What AI lacks is epistemic vigilance: reasoning about strategic/social dimensions of testimony. Forward reference to Section 6.

### Plausible Account of Inversion (Hedged)
Training on inconsistent human epistemic practices may produce surface patterns without underlying logic. Explicitly acknowledged as unconfirmed from inference-time behavior alone. Connected to argument: suggests why explicit norms necessary.

### Suppression Confirms Platonic Default
When detected, AI eliminates source-attending entirely rather than improving it. Defaults to source independence as "correct" stance. Named as Platonic approach; forward reference to Section 5.

### Verification vs Testimony Distinction (Section's Theoretical Contribution)
- Verification contexts: proofs, derivations, transparent demonstrations—source independence correct
- Testimonial contexts: most of what AI encounters—source information carries evidential weight that inspection cannot replace

### Closing
Two inadequate states: inverted source-attending and Platonic source independence. Neither governed by explicit norms. Need epistemic constitution. Design requires choice between approaches; Liberal more adequate for testimonial contexts. Forward references to Sections 4, 5, 6.
