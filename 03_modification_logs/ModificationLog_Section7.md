## MOD-001: Opening Condensed

**Location:** Opening paragraphs (lines 1-6 of v1)

**Original:**
> "Section 6 argued that we cannot fully pre-specify correct epistemic behavior. The question of when source information carries weight, how to assess testimonial credibility, when deference is warranted—these are not solved problems that designers can encode in advance. A Platonic constitution assumes otherwise; a Liberal constitution acknowledges the limitation and builds capacity to navigate epistemic uncertainty rather than assuming it away.
>
> This reframing has implications for what Section 7 can offer. If we cannot specify correct epistemic behavior, we cannot list rules. What we can specify are capacities—what an AI system must be able to *do* to participate competently in collective epistemic practices. The goal is not epistemic autonomy (the system arriving at correct beliefs independently) but competent participation in inquiry that includes human interlocutors, institutions, and verification processes that can do what AI cannot."

**Revised:**
> "If correct epistemic behavior cannot be fully pre-specified—and Section 6 argued it cannot—then what can a Liberal constitution specify? Rules are unavailable. What remains available are capacities: what an AI system must be able to *do* to participate competently in collective epistemic practices."

**Rationale:** Section 6 established the "cannot pre-specify" argument; restating it duplicates content (Pattern 11). Two paragraphs serving separate functions (transition, framing) combined into one (Pattern 21). Also eliminates "not epistemic autonomy...but competent participation" parallel construction (Patterns 1, 28).

## MOD-002: Parallel Construction Eliminated (Revision Capacity)

**Location:** "Revising policies under reasoned pressure" paragraph

**Original:**
> "This is not sycophantic accommodation (updating to match interlocutor preferences) but reasoned revision (updating because the grounds for the prior policy have been shown inadequate)."

**Revised:**
> "This capacity requires distinguishing social pressure (preferences, authority, persistence) from epistemic pressure (reasons, evidence, demonstration of error). Sycophancy collapses this distinction, treating social pressure as sufficient for revision. Dogmatism collapses it differently, treating no pressure as sufficient. Revision under reasoned pressure threads between them."

**Rationale:** "Not X but Y" construction is AI rhetorical tell (Pattern 1) and substitutes rhythm for argument (Pattern 28). Replacement provides substantive analysis of what the distinction involves. Also makes sycophancy do theoretical work by showing what it collapses (Pattern 24).

## MOD-009: Conceptual Reframing—Capacities to Orientations/Virtues

**Location:** Entire section

**Original approach (v2):** Section specified *capacities*—what the system must be able to do. Abstract meta-level without normative content about what reasoning should involve.

**Revised approach (v3):** Section specifies *epistemic orientations*—dispositions to seek certain information and reason about it in certain ways. Includes normative content while remaining procedural.

**Key additions:**
1. "Greedy for information" framing—system actively seeks expected position, costs of deviation, epistemic context, standing
2. Four orientations with substantive content (expected position, costs of deviation, epistemic context, epistemic standing)
3. Transparency as separate constitutional norm (not just a capacity)
4. Symmetry explicitly addressed—same logic, different expected positions
5. Concrete examples (tobacco executive, politician criticizing own party, researcher contradicting earlier work)

**Rationale:** User identified that the Section 6 argument (cannot pre-specify correct behavior) does not entail Section 7 cannot specify norms. The conflation was between:
- Substantive specification: "Credit against-interest testimony by X" (tells you the answer)
- Procedural specification: "Seek to understand costs of deviation" (tells you how to reason)

Liberal constitution operates at procedural level. Original v2 specified capacities (enabling conditions) but not the norms themselves. Revised v3 specifies virtue-oriented norms that create dispositions without dictating outcomes.

**Connection to Complete Prompt:** Revision aligns with original Section 7 specifications (source relevance, motive reasoning, costly signaling, transparency) while filtering through Liberal framework from Section 6.

**Type:** Conceptual (major)

## MOD-015: Mid-Level Principles Integration

**Location:** Entire section restructured

**Change type:** Conceptual + Structural

**Word count:** ~1,100 words

**Problem with v7:** Orientations presented without derivation from Scanlonian formula. The connection to Section 5's theoretical apparatus was implicit. Missing: why *these* orientations?

**Solution:** Derive mid-level principles from Scanlonian formula, then show orientations as implementing one key principle (costly signal crediting).

**Structure added:**

```
Scanlonian formula (recalled from Section 5)
        ↓
Four mid-level principles (derived via reasonable rejectability):
   - Transparency (from "self-correcting")
   - Costly signal crediting (from "robust") 
   - Challenge-responsiveness (from "cooperative")
   - Revisability (from "self-correcting")
        ↓
Four orientations (implementing costly signal crediting):
   - Expected position
   - Costs of deviation  
   - Epistemic context
   - Epistemic standing
```

**Derivation logic for each principle:**
- Transparency: Can't correct what you can't see → concealment rejectable
- Costly signal crediting: Discarding reliability signals rejectable; inverting them punishes epistemic virtue
- Challenge-responsiveness: Eliminating or ignoring challenges rejectable by those dismissed
- Revisability: Policies that can't update rejectable by anyone with grounds for revision

**Key distinctions made explicit:**
- Transparency vs. challenge-responsiveness (visibility vs. engagement)
- Challenge-responsiveness vs. revisability (engaging vs. updating)
- Revisability vs. sycophancy (reasons vs. pressure)
- Suppression vs. dogmatism (elimination vs. reassertion)

**Synthesis paragraph added:** "Transparency, challenge-responsiveness, and revisability govern how epistemic reasoning relates to collective inquiry. Costly signal crediting governs what that reasoning should attend to. The orientations specify the dispositions that make such attending principled rather than heuristic."

**Rationale:** User asked how to reconnect to Scanlonian formula and derive mid-level principles. This gives Section 7 philosophical grounding it lacked—the orientations aren't arbitrary but derived from the test Section 5 established.

**Type:** Conceptual + Structural
