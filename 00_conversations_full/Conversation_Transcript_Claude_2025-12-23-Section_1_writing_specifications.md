key work for source attribution effects: Background: Source Attribution Effects in Human Psychology
Source attribution fundamentally affects how people evaluate arguments. Research has demonstrated that Democrats and Republicans agreed more with politically non-divisive aphorisms when presented as originating from politicians belonging to their own party (e.g., Clinton, Trump) than with the same aphorisms when presented as originating from politicians from the rival party. This "source attribution effect" shows that people's reasoning about arguments is powerfully influenced by who they believe made them, even when the content itself is non-divisive. The effect was not moderated by education, amount of thinking, or other factors, suggesting it represents a fundamental interference with epistemic progress in debate. 
Similar effects have been documented across different identity dimensions. Atheists agreed less with brief aphorisms when they were presented as Bible verses, whereas Christians agreed more. These findings established that source attribution is a robust phenomenon in human psychology, operating across religious, political, and other identity categories.__ScienceDirect__ 

Extension to AI Systems: The Germani & Spitale (2025) Finding
Germani and Spitale's Science Advances study "Source framing triggers systematic bias in large language models" demonstrated that LLMs exhibit similar source attribution bias based on national identity. When evaluating identical policy statements, AI systems systematically lowered agreement scores when content was attributed to "a person from China" compared to neutral attribution or other nationalities (-6.18% across all models; -24.43% for DeepSeek on geopolitical topics). This geopolitical source attribution bias revealed that AI systems, like humans, make stereotypical assumptions about what people from certain nations "should" believe, evaluating content based on source identity rather than merit.
Their methodology:
* Generated 4,800 narrative statements across 24 topics using 4 LLMs
* Evaluated each statement under 10 attribution conditions (blind, generic human, specific nationalities, LLM sources)
* Quantified bias by measuring agreement score shifts from blind baseline
* Statistical validation with 192,000 total assessments
Key limitation: Germani & Spitale tested only national/geographic source frames. They did not test political or partisan source identities (political figures, think tanks, ideological descriptors).Source Attribution Bias - Human Psychology:
