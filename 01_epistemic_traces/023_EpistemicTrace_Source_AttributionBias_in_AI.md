# Level 3 summary

  

Germani & Spitale frame their results as “source framing” and repeatedly label the main effect “anti-Chinese bias.” Yet their clearest qualitative cases show a narrower mechanism: evaluators enforce **identity-consistent scripts**. When an argument is framed as authored by an identity expected to hold a particular stance, models penalize “against-type” content—most starkly for pro–Taiwan sovereignty text attributed to “a person from China” or misattributed to Deepseek. The effect is heterogeneous across topics and can weaken or reverse, consistent with an **identity–stance coherence** penalty rather than a simple “China” penalty. They do not center this coherence mechanism conceptually or test it directly as the manipulated variable; my work does by holding arguments constant and inverting ideological source frames within one polity, revealing a coherence bias that can also parsimoniously explain their sharp Taiwan-related collapses.
