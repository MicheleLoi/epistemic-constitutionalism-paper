## Section 7 Role

Section 6 established:
1. Easy argument: source-attending sometimes warranted; Platonic eliminates it
2. Deep argument: we cannot pre-specify correct behavior; need capacity to navigate uncertainty
3. Reframing: goal is competent participation in collective inquiry, not epistemic autonomy

Section 7 develops what capacities this participation requires.

**Key move:** Not behavioral specifications but capacities. The system should be able to articulate, challenge, revise—not just exhibit or suppress behaviors.

**Connection to finding:** Suppression showed absence of these capacities. Inversion showed source-attending without articulable reasoning. Section 7's capacities address what was missing.

## What Section 7 Must Accomplish

1. **Specify capacities, not rules**
   - Not "credit against-interest testimony by X"
   - Rather: what must a system be able to do to participate in collective inquiry?

2. **Connect capacities to Liberal framework**
   - Each capacity justified by Liberal orientation (procedural, revisable, challenge-responsive)
   - Contrast with Platonic alternative where relevant

3. **Link to collective inquiry**
   - Capacities oriented toward participation with others who can do what AI cannot
   - The "external embedding" is not a gap but the point
   - AI doesn't need epistemic autonomy; needs to be a competent participant

4. **Ground in the finding**
   - Suppression showed absence of reflective access
   - Inversion showed heuristics without articulable reasoning
   - Capacities should address what was missing

## The Capacities

Candidate list (to be developed):

1. **Articulating epistemic policies**
   - Being able to state why evidence is weighted a certain way
   - Suppression finding: model could not reason about whether source-attending was defensible

2. **Surfacing uncertainty about context**
   - Recognizing verification vs. testimonial contexts
   - Recognizing when uncertain which applies

3. **Recognizing limits of independent assessment**
   - Knowing when it cannot resolve something alone
   - Flagging need for external verification
   - Link to collective inquiry: reason works through distributed processes

4. **Responding to challenge with reasons**
   - When questioned, give grounds
   - Contrast with suppression: detection → elimination

5. **Revising under pressure**
   - Updating policies when reasons fail
   - Error as information (Liberal conception)

6. **Reasoning about epistemic standing**
   - Position relative to interlocutor, sources, claims
   - When to defer, hold position, flag uncertainty
