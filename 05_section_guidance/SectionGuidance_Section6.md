## Section 6 Role

Section 5 characterized two approaches without resolving the choice. Section 6 argues that for testimonial contexts, the Liberal approach is more adequate.

**Key move:** The argument is not that Liberal is always correct, but that it is more adequate for the contexts AI systems typically encounter (testimonial, not verification).

**Connection to finding:** The asymmetric source effects show unprincipled source-attending. The question is how to respond. Section 6 argues Liberal reconstruction (make source-attending principled) is more adequate than Platonic correction (eliminate source-attending).

---

## What Section 6 Must Accomplish

1. **Introduce epistemic vigilance** (Mercier's specific mechanism)
   - Reasoning about *why* someone is telling you something
   - Strategic/social dimensions of testimony
   - This is what AI lacks (Section 3 named the absence; Section 6 develops it)

2. **Introduce costly signaling logic**
   - Against-interest testimony is more credible
   - Cost of deviation from expected position
   - This should produce *symmetric* effects across ideological positions

3. **Connect to the finding**
   - Asymmetry shows no costly signaling logic operating
   - AI inverts the logic (penalizes rather than credits against-interest testimony)
   - This is evidence of implicit heuristics, not principled reasoning

4. **Argue Liberal more adequate for testimonial contexts**
   - Verification contexts (proofs, derivations): Platonic correct
   - Testimonial contexts (most of what AI encounters): source information carries evidential weight
   - Liberal allows principled source-attending; Platonic eliminates it

5. **Why Liberal for Testimonial Contexts** (~300 words)  
   Verification vs. testimony distinction (from Section 3). Liberal allows what testimony requires; Platonic eliminates it. Forward reference to Section 7.
