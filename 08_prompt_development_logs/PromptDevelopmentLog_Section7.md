## Decision Log

### PDL-S7-001: Rules Incompatible with Section 6 Argument

**Issue:** Complete Prompt specified Section 7 as "Source-attending norms (the sketch)" with "Concrete normative proposals." But Section 6's core argument is that we cannot pre-specify correct epistemic behavior. Listing rules would contradict this.

**Source in conversation:**

> "what do you foresee section 7 to contain?"

**Analysis performed:**

> "Based on how Section 6 has developed, Section 7 faces a tension. The original plan (from guidance): sketch source-attending norms. But if the argument is that we can't pre-specify correct behavior, Section 7 can't just list rules. That would be Platonic."

**Decision:** Section 7 cannot provide behavioral specifications. The original Complete Prompt specification must be modified.

**Affects:** Section 7 Guidance; modification to Complete Prompt Section VI.

---

### PDL-S7-002: Capacities as Alternative Framing

**Issue:** If not rules, what should Section 7 contain?

**Source in conversation:**

> "would it be more coherent if it talks about capacities that AI should be endowed with, in order to match the requirements of a liberal constitution?"

**Analysis:**

Capacities resolve the tension:
- Capacities specify what the system must be able to *do*, not what outputs are correct
- A capacity to articulate epistemic policies is not a rule about what policies to have
- A capacity to respond to challenge is not a specification of correct responses
- This is consistent with "we don't know correct behavior"—capacities enable navigation of uncertainty

**Capacities identified:**
1. Articulating epistemic policies
2. Surfacing uncertainty about context
3. Recognizing limits of independent assessment
4. Responding to challenge with reasons
5. Revising under pressure
6. Reasoning about epistemic standing

**Decision:** Section 7 specifies capacities for participation in collective inquiry, not behavioral rules.

**Affects:** Section 7 Guidance (core reframing).

---

### PDL-S7-003: External Embedding as the Point

**Issue:** Complete Prompt Section XIII distinguished "internal norms" (how AI reasons) from "external norms" (how AI is embedded in collective practices), deferring external to Limitations. How does Section 7's reframing relate to this?

**Source in conversation:**

> "And could that be a link to what we announced was a perspective to be excluded (look at the full prompt), namely the idea that epistemic grounding needs a relation with other agents, typically but not necessarily humans, that can interact with the world in ways other than language prediction?"

**Analysis performed:**

> "The 'external embedding' that was deferred becomes not a separate topic but the *point* of the capacities. Liberal constitution isn't about making AI epistemically autonomous. It's about making AI able to participate in the distributed epistemic processes that make reasoning work—the processes Mercier says are necessary for reason to function."

**Connection:**
- Internal capacities are *oriented toward* external participation
- They are not separate dimensions but connected
- The capacities are what enable participation in collective inquiry
- AI doesn't need epistemic autonomy; needs to be a competent participant

**Decision:** 
- Section 7 specifies capacities (internal) with their orientation toward participation (external) made explicit
- External embedding is reframed as the point, not a gap to note
- Full development of external embedding still deferred to Limitations, but now coherently: Limitations addresses what participation requires beyond the capacities Section 7 specifies

**Affects:** Section 7 Guidance; modification to Complete Prompt Section XIII framing.
