## Decision Log

### PDL-S6-001: Mercier Distribution Clarification

**Issue:** Initial Section 5 draft introduced Mercier's argumentative theory of reason. But Complete Prompt (PDL-011) specifies "Section 6 (Why Liberal) introduces epistemic vigilance." Was this overlap or conflict?

**Source in conversation:**

> "One potential tension: I introduce Mercier's argumentative theory of reason in Section 5 to motivate the distinction. But Section 6 is supposed to draw on Mercier's epistemic vigilance to argue for Liberal."

**Analysis performed:**

> "The Complete Prompt uses 'Mercier' to refer to two distinct things without distinguishing them:
> 1. Mercier's general theory: Reason is social/argumentative, evolved for persuasion and coordination, works best collectively. This grounds why Liberal is a *coherent* approach.
> 2. Mercier's specific mechanism: Epistemic vigilance—reasoning about *why* someone tells you something, costly signaling, against-interest testimony. This argues why Liberal is *more adequate* for testimonial contexts."

**Verification:**

> "PDL-011 says 'introduces epistemic vigilance'—not 'introduces Mercier.' The general theory is not epistemic vigilance."

**Decision:** Clarify in Section Guidance that:
- Section 5: General theory via Mercier & Sperber (2017) - reason as social/argumentative
- Section 6: Epistemic vigilance via Mercier (2020) *Not Born Yesterday* - introduced fresh, not mentioned in Section 5

These are two levels of the same framework. The general theory grounds why Liberal is coherent; the specific mechanism argues why Liberal is more adequate for testimonial contexts.

**Affects:** Section Guidance for Section 6; proposed modification to Complete Prompt Section V.

### PDL-S6-003: Section 6 Structure from Argument Flow

**Issue:** What should Section 6 accomplish given Section 5's setup?

**Source:** Complete Prompt Section II (Core Argument Architecture), Step 5:

> "Resolution: The empirical finding shows why platonic fails. Source information *matters* epistemically (Mercier's epistemic vigilance). The question is not whether to attend to sources but *how*. Liberal norms—including principled source-attending based on costly signaling—are needed."

**Source:** Complete Prompt Section V (Theoretical Commitments), "Epistemic Vigilance as the Key Addition":

> "Proper source-attending requires reasoning about:
> 1. Source's expected position (what would they normally say?)
> 2. Cost of deviation (what does it cost them to argue this?)
> 3. Costly signaling principle: against-interest testimony is more credible because speaker incurs costs
> 
> This should be *symmetric* across ideological positions. The empirical finding shows current AI is asymmetric—suggesting no principled costly signaling reasoning, just implicit heuristics."

**Decision:** Section 6 must:
1. Introduce epistemic vigilance (what AI lacks, named in Section 3)
2. Introduce costly signaling logic (symmetric requirement)
3. Connect to finding (asymmetry as evidence of missing logic)
4. Argue Liberal more adequate for testimonial contexts

**Affects:** Section Guidance for Section 6 (What Section 6 Must Accomplish).
