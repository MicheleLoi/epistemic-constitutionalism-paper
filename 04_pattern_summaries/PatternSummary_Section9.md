## Generalizable Patterns Extracted

### Pattern 42: Opening-Closing Echo as Redundancy

**Trigger:** Conclusion opens with a claim and closes with what feels like a distinct claim.

**Problem:** Conclusions often restate the paper's central point at both beginning and end. This feels like framing but is redundancy. The two statements do the same work.

**Example from Section 9:**
- Opening: "AI systems reason epistemically but lack explicit norms"
- Closing of second sentence: "implicit epistemic policy to make explicit"
- Same claim, different words.

**Solution:** Choose one location for the claim. In conclusions, the opening can assume the paper's work; it doesn't need to re-announce the gap.

**Generalization:** Conclusions are high-risk for redundancy because they synthesize. Watch for opening and closing doing the same work in different words.

**Application scope:** All conclusions; any synthesizing section.

---

### Pattern 43: Conclusions Cannot Introduce Contrasts

**Trigger:** Conclusion uses a distinction or contrast not developed in the body.

**Problem:** "Principled rather than heuristic" appeared in conclusion, but the paper never discussed heuristics. The contrast felt natural but wasn't earned.

**Solution:** Conclusions can only invoke distinctions developed in the body. If the contrast wasn't part of the argument, it cannot appear in the synthesis.

**Generalization:** Conclusions synthesize; they do not introduce. Any conceptual content in a conclusion must trace to prior sections.

**Application scope:** All conclusions; especially when summarizing normative proposals.

---

### Pattern 44: Pattern Violations Intensify in Conclusions

**Trigger:** Patterns from earlier sections (e.g., Pattern 28) appear in conclusion.

**Problem:** The "not X but Y" construction (Pattern 28) appeared despite being flagged in Section 6. Conclusions are written quickly as synthesis—pattern vigilance drops.

**Solution:** Actively check conclusions against cumulative pattern list. The temptation to use rhetorical flourishes is highest when wrapping up.

**Generalization:** Conclusions are high-risk for pattern violations. The synthesis mindset invites exactly the constructions earlier sections worked to eliminate.

**Application scope:** All conclusions; final review checkpoint.

---

### Pattern 45: Compression Reveals Padding

**Trigger:** Section compresses significantly (670 → 390 words) without losing content.

**Problem:** The longer version felt complete. But compression removed 280 words with no loss of substance—revealing that the extra words were padding, not content.

**Solution:** For conclusions especially, draft long, then compress aggressively. What survives is the actual content. What disappears was elaboration mistaken for argument.

**Generalization:** If aggressive compression loses nothing, the original was padded. Conclusions should be lean; most can lose 30-40% without loss.

**Application scope:** All conclusions; any section that feels "about right" length-wise.
