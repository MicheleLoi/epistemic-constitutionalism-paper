## Generalizable Patterns Extracted

### Pattern 34: Derive Rather Than List Normative Content

**Trigger:** Section must provide normative proposals (principles, norms, rules).

**Problem:** Listing norms without derivation appears arbitrary. Reader asks: why these norms and not others?

**Solution:** Show derivation from established theoretical apparatus. In this case: Scanlonian formula → mid-level principles → orientations. Each principle traceable to component of formula ("self-correcting" → transparency, revisability; "cooperative" → challenge-responsiveness; "robust" → costly signal crediting).

**Generalization:** Normative sections should exhibit their derivational structure. The principles aren't discovered; they're derived. Showing the derivation is the argument.

**Application scope:** Any section proposing norms, principles, or requirements; especially in papers with established theoretical frameworks.

---

### Pattern 35: Distinguish Adjacent Concepts Explicitly

**Trigger:** Multiple concepts occupy nearby conceptual space and could be conflated.

**Problem:** Reader may not see why both concepts are needed, or may confuse them.

**Solution:** State distinctions explicitly. Examples from Section 7:
- Transparency vs. challenge-responsiveness (visibility vs. engagement)
- Challenge-responsiveness vs. revisability (engaging vs. updating)
- Revisability vs. sycophancy (reasons vs. pressure)
- Suppression vs. dogmatism (elimination vs. reassertion)

**Generalization:** When concepts are adjacent, the distinctions are doing philosophical work. Make them explicit rather than hoping the reader infers them.

**Application scope:** Any section introducing multiple related concepts; especially taxonomic or principled sections.

---

### Pattern 36: Show Failure Modes for Each Principle

**Trigger:** Principle or norm is proposed.

**Problem:** Abstract principles can seem disconnected from concrete problems the paper addresses.

**Solution:** For each principle, identify the failure mode—what happens when violated. Ground in the paper's findings where possible. Examples:
- Transparency → suppression finding (hidden reasoning)
- Costly signal crediting → inversion finding (penalizing virtue)
- Challenge-responsiveness → suppression (elimination) or dogmatism (reassertion)
- Revisability → sycophancy (pressure-updating) or dogmatism (no updating)

**Generalization:** Principles gain traction when paired with failure modes. The finding becomes evidence for the principle by showing what goes wrong without it.

**Application scope:** Any normative section; especially when empirical findings are available to ground the principles.

### Pattern 37: Structural Continuity via Pickup

**Trigger:** Section follows another section that ended with a list or preview.

**Problem:** New section risks either ignoring the setup (discontinuity) or repeating it verbatim (redundancy).

**Solution:** Pick up the prior section's closing and develop it. Section 7 opens: "Section 6 concluded that a Liberal constitution requires capacities enabling participation in collective inquiry: articulating epistemic policies, surfacing uncertainty..." This echoes Section 6's closing list, then announces development.

**Generalization:** When prior section previews what's coming, the new section should explicitly pick it up—acknowledge and extend, not repeat or ignore.

**Application scope:** Any section following a section with forward reference to it.

### Pattern 38: Hierarchical Normative Structure

**Trigger:** Section must specify norms at different levels of abstraction.

**Problem:** Flat list of norms lacks organization. Reader can't see relationships.

**Solution:** Organize hierarchically:
- High level: Test or formula (Scanlonian reasonable rejectability)
- Mid level: Principles derived from test (transparency, costly signal crediting, etc.)
- Low level: Orientations/dispositions implementing principles

Then state relationships: "Transparency, challenge-responsiveness, and revisability govern how epistemic reasoning relates to collective inquiry. Costly signal crediting governs what that reasoning should attend to. The orientations specify the dispositions that make such attending principled."

**Generalization:** Normative content benefits from explicit hierarchy. The synthesis showing relationships is as important as the norms themselves.

**Application scope:** Any section with multiple normative elements at different abstraction levels.

### Pattern 39: Style Alignment Across Sections

**Trigger:** Section follows sections with established stylistic conventions.

**Problem:** Style drift across sections makes paper feel fragmented. Reader adjusts expectations, then is surprised.

**Solution:** Examine prior sections (especially immediately preceding) and match:
- Header frequency and formatting
- Paragraph length and rhythm
- Cross-reference style (conversational vs. parenthetical)
- Prose density

Section 7 v7 aligned with Section 6: flowing prose, minimal headers, natural cross-references.

**Generalization:** Style is part of continuity. Match the established conventions unless there's reason to deviate.

**Application scope:** All sections after the first few establish conventions.

---

### Pattern 40: Compression-Restoration Awareness

**Trigger:** Draft undergoes multiple revisions with different goals (density, economy, style).

**Problem:** Revisions can work against each other. Compression loses content; restoration bloats; neither achieves the goal.

**Solution:** Track what each revision optimizes for. When goals conflict, identify the priority:
- v4 optimized for density
- v5 optimized for economy (too compressed)
- v6 restored density but wrong style
- v7 aligned style
- v8 added derivational structure

The final version should achieve the highest-priority goals. Lower-priority goals may be sacrificed.

**Generalization:** Multi-revision processes need meta-awareness of what each pass is for. Avoid undoing good work from prior passes.

**Application scope:** Any section undergoing multiple substantial revisions.

---

### Pattern 41: Format as Argument

**Trigger:** Proposing norms that could be implemented in existing frameworks.

**Problem:** Abstract norms seem disconnected from practice. Reader thinks: how would this actually work?

**Solution:** Render proposals in the format of existing implementations. Section 7's principles use "Choose the response that..." matching Anthropic's constitutional format. The format itself demonstrates implementability—the extension from ethics to epistemics isn't just conceptual.

**Generalization:** When proposals extend existing frameworks, adopt the existing framework's format. The formal parallel makes the extension concrete.

**Application scope:** Any normative proposal that extends or complements existing implemented systems.
