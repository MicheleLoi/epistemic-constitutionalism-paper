## Generalizable Patterns Extracted

### Pattern 1: AI Rhetorical Tell Elimination

**Trigger:** "Not because X, but because Y" construction

**Problem:** This parallel negation-affirmation structure has become a signature AI writing pattern. It creates false symmetry and substitutes rhythmic balance for actual insight.

**Solution:** Rewrite as direct statement. Original: "Not because the logic changes, but because the costly signal changes." Revised: "The logic remains identical; the costly signal differs, and that difference is epistemically relevant."

**Generalization:** Avoid constructions that use parallel structure to appear insightful. If the insight is real, it survives straightforward expression.

**Application scope:** All sections; vigilance required throughout.

---

### Pattern 2: Citation Integration Without Genuflection

**Trigger:** Need to cite foundational work

**Problem:** AI tendency toward excessive literature genuflection ("The seminal work of X established..."; "Building on the important contribution of Y...")

**Solution:** Integrate citations as factual anchors, not praise. Example: "Just as Anthropic's Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) introduced explicit principles..." — the citation serves orientation, not homage.

**Generalization:** Citations should ground claims, not demonstrate awareness of literature. Treat references as evidence, not credentials.

**Application scope:** All sections; especially relevant for Section 4 (literature placement paragraph).

---

### Pattern 3: Literature Positioning as Extension

**Trigger:** Introducing prior work (Germani & Spitale) that our study extends

**Problem:** Risk of either (a) over-claiming novelty by minimizing prior work, or (b) undermining contribution by over-emphasizing continuity.

**Solution:** Acknowledge prior work precisely, identify specific limitation, state extension clearly. Example: "Their study tested national and geographic source frames but not political or partisan identities. Our study extends this work to partisan source attribution."

**Generalization:** When building on prior work, the three-move structure works: (1) what they did, (2) what they didn't do, (3) what we do.

**Application scope:** Section 2 (methods comparison with Germani & Spitale); Section 4 (epistemic responsibility literature).

---

### Pattern 4: Empirical Finding as Attractor

**Trigger:** Structuring introduction

**Problem:** AI tendency to lead with theoretical framework, treating empirical work as illustration.

**Solution:** Lead with the finding. The theory serves the finding, not vice versa. Structure: hook → concept → finding → interpretation → framework → contribution.

**Generalization:** In empirically-grounded conceptual papers, the data is the proof of work. Don't bury it in service of abstraction.

**Application scope:** All sections; maintain throughout.

---

### Pattern 5: Roadmap Without Repetition

**Trigger:** Final paragraph of introduction

**Problem:** Roadmaps often repeat section titles and content already stated, creating redundancy.

**Solution:** Roadmap should be minimal—one sentence per section indicating *what happens*, not restating *what is argued*. Example: "Section 2 presents the empirical finding in detail" not "Section 2 presents our empirical finding that source attribution effects exist, are asymmetric, and are suppressed under meta-awareness."

**Generalization:** Trust the reader. The roadmap orients; it doesn't preview.

**Application scope:** Section 1 only (roadmap written); reference for conclusion structure.
